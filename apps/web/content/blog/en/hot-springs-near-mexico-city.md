A day in the water is one of the plans that always works near Mexico City, in cold weather or hot: a warm spring in Cuautla, a clear river in Tlaltizapán and thermal towns in the south of the State of Mexico. This guide sorts them by drive time. Events in these towns for the next 30 days appear below, in case your visit lands on a fair.

Long weekends fill up. A weekday, or a Saturday if you leave before 8:00, is a different experience.

<!-- eventos -->

## Under 2 hours: Morelos

### Cuautla: 1 h 50

[Cuautla](/municipio/cuautla) is a city of springs. Agua Hedionda is the best known: the water comes out warm and sulfurous, and there are pools for spending the day. It is not a quiet spa. It is a town balneario, with families, food and noise. That is the plan.

In December the city holds the Nochebuena fair. The 2026 dates are in [Christmas near Mexico City](/en/blog/christmas-near-mexico-city-2026).

### Oaxtepec, in Yautepec: under 2 h

Oaxtepec belongs to [Yautepec](/municipio/yautepec) and has the big vacation park, with several pools and plenty of room for kids. It is the simplest plan if you don't want to wander a town: you arrive, you pay, you stay. The town of Yautepec, next door, has its own square if you want to eat outside the park.

### Las Estacas, Tlaltizapán: about 2 h

[Tlaltizapán de Zapata](/municipio/tlaltizapan-de-zapata) is south of Cuernavaca. Las Estacas is a clear spring-fed river, not a pool: you swim with the current and walk back along the bank. There is an entrance fee. Bring a life vest if someone in your group doesn't swim, and a layer for the shade, because the water feels cool even on a hot day.

## About 2 h 30: Ixtapan de la Sal and Tonatico

The two towns sit next to each other, so you don't have to pick just one.

[Ixtapan de la Sal](/municipio/ixtapan-de-la-sal) lives off its thermal water. There are large bathhouses, with indoor pools, and simpler ones in town. It is a Pueblo Mágico: the center takes a short walk and the real plan is the water.

[Tonatico](/municipio/tonatico), about ten minutes away, has a municipal balneario, the **Grutas de la Estrella** caves and the **Tzumpantitlán** waterfall, with a park around it. With kids, this is the fullest day on the list: water, a cave and a fall, without changing region.

## If you have a long day: Chignahuapan

[Chignahuapan](/municipio/chignahuapan), in Puebla's Sierra Norte, is about three hours away. Besides the Christmas-ornament workshops, there are hot springs on the edge of town. It makes sense if you were already going to the sierra, or if you want water plus a town to sleep in. As a there-and-back day from Mexico City, the drive eats the morning.

## Before you leave

- **Leave early on Saturday.** The México–Cuautla highway and the roads south into the State of Mexico fill up from mid-morning.
- **Ask about the temperature** if you go in winter. "Thermal" does not always mean hot. In Cuautla the water is warm; at Las Estacas it is cool.
- **Bring cash**, a swimsuit, a towel and sandals. Several parks don't rent everything.
- **Check the town's page** in case a fair falls on the same day. A fair plus a water park is a lot for one afternoon.
