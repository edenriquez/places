Near Mexico City you eat by town, not by trend. Each place has a dish people from the region drive out for, and several give it a fair once a year. This guide splits what you can eat any Saturday from the fairs, which do have dates. Confirmed events in these towns appear below.

Bring cash. At a town market, a stall or a parking lot, cards are still the exception.

## Any weekend

### Barbacoa in Tlalmanalco: 1 h 10

[Tlalmanalco](/municipio/tlalmanalco) is a pit-barbacoa stop on the way to the volcanoes. Eat at the market, early: arrive mid-morning if you want a choice. The 16th-century open chapel of the former convent is a few blocks away and takes only a few minutes.

### Mole in Milpa Alta, inside the city

San Pedro Atocpan, in [Milpa Alta](/municipio/milpa-alta), smells like mole from the edge of town. There are moles made with different chiles, in a taco, on a plate and as paste to take home. It is inside the city, so you are not depending on a highway. In October the town holds the **Feria Nacional del Mole**; the rest of the year the stalls stay open.

### The market in Amecameca and the bread in Ozumba

[Amecameca](/municipio/amecameca), about 1 h 15 away, has an honest town market: quesadillas, cecina and antojitos, and from there you walk up the Sacromonte. [Ozumba](/municipio/ozumba), about twenty minutes farther, is known for its **bread**, especially the holiday loaves. In December the bread and the poinsettia share a fair.

### Trout in Malinalco: 2 h 10

In [Malinalco](/municipio/malinalco) trout is raised nearby and served in town restaurants, next to the archaeological site and the convent. It is a long day trip, or a night away. The **trout fair** is in mid-October.

### Cecina in Tenango del Aire: about 1 h

[Tenango del Aire](/municipio/tenango-del-aire) is on the volcano corridor, before Amecameca. Cecina and cheese are the reason to stop, and in October they have a fair. It is a pause more than a town for the whole day: it combines well with Tlalmanalco or Amecameca.

### The Tepoztlán market: 1 h 30

The weekend market in [Tepoztlán](/municipio/tepoztlan) is part of the walk: quesadillas, unusual ice cream flavors and stalls set up on the square. It is not one dish. It is a town for grazing, then the convent or the Tepozteco. The climb is in [hiking near Mexico City](/en/blog/hiking-near-mexico-city).

## Fairs, by month

These festivals repeat every year. The exact day of the current edition, once there is a flyer, shows up in the event list above.

- **October:** Feria Nacional del Mole in Milpa Alta, the trout fair in Malinalco, and the cecina and cheese fair in Tenango del Aire.
- **December:** the poinsettia and bread fair in Ozumba, from the 15th, and the Nochebuena fair in Cuautla. Details are in [Christmas near Mexico City](/en/blog/christmas-near-mexico-city-2026).
- **February:** the tamal fair in Tepoztlán, on the 2nd, Candlemas, and the flower fair in [Tenancingo](/municipio/tenancingo).
- **March:** the pulque and barbacoa fair in Tlalmanalco.
- **July:** the plum and peach fair in [Tetela del Volcán](/municipio/tetela-del-volcan), in the orchards on the slopes of Popocatépetl.
- **August:** the walnut fair in Amecameca.
- **September:** the corn and pulque fair in [Ayapango](/municipio/ayapango), next to Amecameca.

## How to shape the day

One meal is enough for a Saturday if you pick the closest town and head back early. If you want two stops, these sit on the same road:

- **Volcanoes:** Tenango del Aire, Tlalmanalco, Amecameca and Ozumba, in that order as you leave the city.
- **Morelos:** Tepoztlán. The 16th-century convents nearby are a separate day; that guide is in Spanish, [conventos del siglo XVI](/blog/conventos-siglo-xvi-cerca-de-cdmx).

On Sunday, between 4 and 8 pm is the worst time to drive back into the city. Eat early and leave before that window, or stay for dinner and come in after dark.
